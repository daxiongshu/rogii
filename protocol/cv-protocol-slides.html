CV Protocol v5

A nested cross-validation protocol for automated search: thousands of experiments without overfitting CV.

How it works, step by step, and why each step is there

The problem: model-selection bias

CV is unbiased for a fixed pipeline. It stops being unbiased once you use it to select one.

What goes wrong

Train four variants, score them all on the held fold, keep the best, report that score.

The number you report is no longer the score of a model. It is the maximum over variants of a noisy quantity — and the maximum of noise is biased upward.

With four variants the bias is small. With the hundreds an automated search produces, it is not.

Why pooling does not fix it

The held fold was used twice — once to choose, once to report — but counted once.

Every fold does the same thing, so pooling five folds does not average the bias away. It pools five biased estimates.

No data is left with which to measure the bias — it was consumed by the selection.

36,450 trials is not a compute problem. It is a selection-bias problem.

Design principle

Bias is treated as a function of how many times the holdout is queried, not how large the search is. Search is unbounded; holdout queries are budgeted.

Search is unbounded

Inspect errors and tune anything on the development folds. Estimates here are recorded as development evidence and never as promotion evidence.

Measurement is rationed

One protected reveal per batch of work. Opening it spends it, and a spent result cannot be reused as confirmation.

Promotion is human

No automatic gate moves the baseline. A person reviews the result and approves it, or it does not move.

Budget the queries, not the search.
Step 1 · Fold

Split by whole group, never by row

The unit is whatever shares information — one subject, one session, one device, one document, one well. Every row of a group lands in the same fold.

Split rows at random and one group straddles train and validation. The model then predicts a row using its own neighbouring rows, which CV cannot detect.

Five folds. Four are open for development. The fifth is sealed as the audit fold.

Three groupings of the same data

Identical rows, different assignment. Weights are estimated under all three and the audit reports regrouping sensitivity, so a result that only holds under one partition shows up as instability.

Read the trio precisely: one unstratified map plus two draws of a single stratified design — same construction, different seed. Their assignments are unrelated, but both equalise the same statistics.

And they equalise them on the target: deciles of peak excursion crossed with endpoint sign. So the axis they balance is the one they cannot probe — only the unstratified map can. Later boundaries balance inference-visible proxies instead and record that no target field was read.

Why: every downstream estimate is conditional on the fold map. A leak here is invisible to everything after it.
Step 2 · Develop

Iterate without limit, against a registered target

On the development folds the agent may read targets, errors, residuals, subgroups and worst cases, and may change architecture, loss, features, augmentation, postprocessing and ensembling. There is no budget.

The protocol pushes harder, not softer: abandoning a mechanism after one or two weak variants is listed as forbidden behaviour, and a trainable family is expected to test at least three meaningful variants.

The staged ladder

  • overfit one fixed batch — an implementation control, not evidence
  • a short run on one inner role
  • a four-fold cross-fit on the development folds
  • confirmation on a second preregistered grouping
  • freeze one global pipeline

Two things that make the freedom safe

Debugging costs nothing statistically. A family must first memorise one fixed batch. Failure there is an implementation result, never CV evidence — so fixing bugs never spends measurement budget.

Holdouts decay on contact. The second grouping is queried only at milestones, and the moment its result changes a decision it is reclassified as development evidence. Before the freeze, a still-untouched grouping is required.

Why: search does not bias the estimate. Selecting on the estimate does.
Step 2b · Readiness

A registered target, and what actually unlocks the freeze

The target is registered before any metric is read

effective_readiness_target = max(prompt_goal, 0.20)

Committed to the ledger when the run is allocated, before development targets or metrics are viewed, and immutable thereafter. Rewriting a registered goal after metrics are seen is on the forbidden list.

Development gain, pooled over F0–F3

for fold k in F0–F3: train on the other three
gain = baseline_rmse − candidate_rmse, pooled on identical rows

Each development group appears exactly once. This is a readiness score, not the protected evaluation — that is the later 20-role procedure.

The target guides effort. It is not a cutoff.

A candidate may advance below the target when its pooled selection-clean gain is positive and the stability evidence justifies spending the audit.

The former reference levels — 3 of 4 folds positive, worst-fold regression 0.03, confirmation gain +0.10, at most 60% gain concentration — are warnings, not limits.

What is genuinely required before revealing

  • pooled clean development gain is positive
  • no evidence of overfitting, no leakage
  • the pipeline is unchanged since the freeze
  • every artifact complete and checksummed
  • a ledger entry authorising this reveal

Positivity is the hard condition. The registered target is not.

Why: a preregistered target sets effort without becoming a threshold to optimise against; the positivity condition stops a reveal being spent on a candidate with nothing to show.
Step 3 · Selection

A fold may not choose anything about itself

Holding a fold out of training is necessary but not sufficient. It must also be held out of every choice made about it.

Four things a fold must not select for itself

  • the checkpoint — which snapshot is used
  • the decoder — how a likelihood becomes an answer
  • the postprocessor — smoothing, extrapolation, clipping
  • the blend weight — how much of the candidate is mixed in

Same-held-fold results are permitted as diagnostics. They are never promotion evidence.

Delegate selection to an inner loop

Inside the four folds that remain, run a second cross-validation. Hold out one more fold, train on the rest, score the variants there.

Four such readings per outer fold, none of which ever touched it. The winner is applied to the held fold once, to produce one prediction.

Its score is then an estimate of one pipeline, not a maximum over variants.

This is the difference between cross-validated and selection-clean.
Step 4 · Roles

25 roles, 15 training runs, 5 deployed

A role is one job: which fold am I predicting, and in whose service. The training set follows from the answer.

  • 5 outer roles — train on four folds, predict the fifth once. These are the only models that ship.
  • 20 inner roles — four per outer fold, ranking the variants without touching the fold they serve.

A role trains on everything except its outer fold and its inner fold — so its data depends only on that unordered pair. Swap the two and the training set is identical.

(outer F4, inner F0)  →  trains F1+F2+F3
(outer F0, inner F4)  →  trains F1+F2+F3

One model, two roles

That single model is held out from both F0 and F4, so it predicts both and fills both roles. Name it by what it excludes — pair {0,4}

Distinct pairs of five folds: C(5,2) = 10. So 20 inner roles need only 10 models.

What it costs

plain (leaky) CV5 runs
independent nesting25 runs
pair-shared nesting15 runs

Three times plain CV, not five. Valid only with identical config and seed — the sharing is bookkeeping, not statistics.

Why: nested CV removes selection bias; pair-sharing cuts its cost from 25 training runs to 15.
Step 5 · Freeze

One pipeline, checksummed, before any labels open

Everything that could be adjusted in response to a result is fixed and hashed first. The same automatic procedure then runs for every outer fold, with no hand edits and no fold-specific fallback.

  • architecture and capacity
  • features, target, loss
  • augmentation and curriculum
  • seeds and the snapshot grid
  • the selector and its tie-break
  • decoder and postprocessor
  • the ensemble weight rule
  • every error-derived heuristic

What is frozen is the rule, not the answer

The selector is fixed. What it selects may legitimately differ per outer fold, because each fold's selector sees a different four-fifths of the data.

Observed in one accepted candidate: the same frozen rule chose decoding temperature 2.0, 2.0, 1.5, 1.5, 1.5 across the five outer folds, and double amplitude on the last one. Nobody chose those by hand.

Deployment completeness

Every component needs a target-free implementation for every outer context. If one is missing, it is either generated for all of them or removed uniformly and the pipeline re-frozen — never patched for a single fold.

Why: anything still adjustable after the reveal can be tuned on the reveal.
Step 6 · Sealed run

Fifteen models, written sealed

The 10 pair models train first and rank the variants for each outer context. Then the 5 outer models train on four folds each and predict their held fold once.

The run writes checkpoints, prediction caches and manifests — all checksummed, all with truth masked and target-derived logs suppressed until every artifact is complete.

Why the run must be sealed, not merely automatic

Pair-sharing means the audit fold's labels are legitimately used mid-run — pair {0,4} serves outer context F0 by validating on F4. Legal, because outer context F0 is entitled to use F4. But it means truth is touched before the reveal, so the seal is what keeps that from reaching a human decision.

What the inner loop ranks

Snapshots are saved on a preregistered epoch grid, and each writes its prediction immediately. Everything else is swept afterwards on those saved predictions — no retraining.

5 snapshots × 5 temperatures × 9 weights
= 225 combinations per training run

This is why 15 runs suffices. The inner layer is not ranking 225 trained models; it is ranking 225 evaluations of 10 trained models.

Axes that require retraining — architecture, loss, seed — are frozen beforehand or multiply the run count.

Why: training is the expensive axis; decoding and blending are free to sweep. Only the free axes are left to selection.
Step 7 · Reveal

All folds at once, and only once

Held-out labels open for every fold simultaneously, with three to five distinct families in the same query — so the best of several queries cannot be reported as a single unbiased estimate.

gain = previous_best − candidate

Compared against the parent frozen before this reveal, on identical folds, rows, masks and metric definition. The reference may not change during the run.

What is reported

  • pooled and per-fold error
  • regrouping sensitivity under both alternative groupings
  • maximum held-group regression
  • weight stability — how many inner weights are positive
  • audit-fold behaviour, reported separately
  • a 2,000-draw paired-group bootstrap on a fixed seed

reveal budget − 1

A revised pipeline needs a genuinely new boundary. A spent result may never serve as its own confirmation.

Why: each additional adaptive query adds bias. One joint query over several families adds it once.
Step 8 · Decide

Three outcomes, and no automatic gate

● Accept

Pooled selection-clean CV beats the frozen parent, every integrity rule passes, and protected evidence shows no material overfitting or transfer reversal.

● Hold

Gain is positive but uncertainty or instability is substantial. Nothing is packaged.

● Reject

Gain is not positive, integrity fails, or the protected evidence materially reverses the development result.

Numeric levels are diagnostics, not gates

Revision 14 demoted the old thresholds — +0.03 pooled, 4 of 5 folds improved, bootstrap 0.80 — from automatic cutoffs to evidence. A fixed threshold becomes an optimisation target. Removing it removes the target.

Hold and Reject are terminal

The spent audit may not be re-queried, and the failed candidate may not be tuned against it. The rejection is preserved rather than rewritten — sealed history is never edited after the fact.

Why: any fixed numeric gate is eventually optimised against.
Step 8b · What is measured

A candidate is never scored alone

The quantity on trial is not the candidate's own error. It is what the candidate adds to the parent that already exists.

gain = parent − ( parent blended with candidate )

Promotion evaluates incremental cross-fitted contribution to the frozen parent ensemble — explicitly not standalone error alone. So a model that is weaker on its own but decorrelated can beat one that is stronger on its own but redundant.

This is standard ensemble behaviour. The protocol makes it the acceptance criterion rather than a modelling tactic.

A sub-target model may still join, if

  • its clean ensemble contribution is positive
  • removing it hurts the frozen ensemble — an ablation, not merely doing no harm
  • its mechanism is materially distinct

Why the weight cannot absorb noise

The blend weight is fitted, so in principle it could absorb noise. Four constraints prevent it.

  • The weight is itself selection-clean. Fitted only inside the held fold’s own four-fold pool. Noise would not transfer.
  • Zero is on the grid. “Do not use this candidate” is always available, and it is what the selector reaches when the inner folds do not support a positive weight.
  • Round down, cap at 0.20. A marginal case is rounded against itself.
  • Weight stability is reported. Positive gain with weight positive in only a few inner estimates is a Hold, not an Accept.

Both outcomes have occurred. One accepted generation was exactly this shape — residual corrections that are meaningless standalone, blended at weight 0.2. In the same run another family reached the audit, was assigned exactly 0.0, and was excluded from the package by arithmetic rather than argument.

Why: the criterion is marginal contribution to the existing ensemble, not standalone error.
Step 9 · Ship

The candidate is mixed in, never swapped in

fold_h = (1 − w_h) · parent_h + w_h · candidate_h
test   = mean(fold_0 … fold_4)

For each outer fold, the weight is estimated only inside that fold's own four-fold pool, under all three groupings. Take the median, round down to a nine-point grid, cap at 0.20, apply unchanged to the held fold.

No full-data refit. The five fold-local weights and five outer checkpoints are what ships.

The frugality is in resolution, not count

One weight per fold is still one free parameter. What changed is how far it may travel: nine rungs, biased downward, with the most optimistic of three estimates discarded by the median.

And the five fold checkpoints are averaged with equal weight — zero free parameters.

There is no unbiased evidence with which to fit them. Out-of-fold gives each group exactly one excluding model, so the five-model average never appears in the record. The structure that makes those weights unfittable is the same one that makes equal weighting the only defensible choice.

Why: every fitted parameter is a query against the data. Reduce pressure with fewer parameters, or with coarser ones.
Step 10 · Approve

Only a human moves the baseline

Promotion builds the private artifact bundle and inference job. Submission spends a submission slot. Neither changes the parent.

The parent registry is updated only by explicit approval after a person reviews whether the clean CV gain and the external score moved together.

The leaderboard firewall

External feedback may never inform model choice, blend weights, or postprocessing. It is monitoring evidence about the validation design, not a signal about the model.

Repeated CV-up / score-down triggers a validation review — the one place the protocol looks at the leaderboard to ask a question about itself.

Why manual, not automatic

Every other decision is automatic so that it cannot be nudged. This one is manual because it is the only irreversible one.

Approving a new parent is the only irreversible act: it changes the reference every future run is measured against. Automating it would let one false positive reset the baseline permanently, and successive false positives would compound with no individual step failing a check.

Promotion and submission are reversible. Moving the baseline is not.

Why: automatic where consistency matters, manual where the action is irreversible.

Why it is designed this way

Six choices, each closing a different route to an optimistic estimate.

Zones, not limits

Unlimited search on one side, a sealed measurement on the other. Overfitting is localised to the side where nothing is claimed.

Delegated choice

No fold selects for itself. The reported number is a measurement, not the argmax of the search that produced it.

Spent on contact

Opening a reveal consumes it. A revised pipeline needs a new boundary, so evidence cannot be re-queried until it agrees.

Shrunk, not tuned

Coarse grids, rounding down, medians over groupings, hard caps. Parameters keep their count but lose their resolution.

Diagnostics, not gates

No fixed numeric threshold to optimise against — judgement over a full evidence panel instead of a single passing score.

Human at the hinge

Everything automatic except the one irreversible act, which is where accountability matters more than consistency.

Each trades reported performance for reliability of the estimate.

Known limit

The budget is one reveal per batch, six batches, five spent. When a boundary is used up the escape valve is a fresh target-free grouping — legitimate, and also the mechanism by which the holdout supply could become unbounded.

The audit fold is prospectively protected, not historically pristine, which is why the official result is labelled selection-clean, development-exposed rather than unbiased.

Report the qualifier with the number, not beneath it.
